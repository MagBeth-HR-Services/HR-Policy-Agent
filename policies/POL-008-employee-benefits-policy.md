> This is a fictional policy created for an educational AI project. It does not describe a real company and does not provide legal, tax, immigration, medical, or financial advice.

## 1. Purpose

Provide general rules for benefits eligibility, enrollment, status checks, qualifying life events, and escalation while preserving plan-document authority.

This policy is intended to give employees, managers, and the HR support system a consistent source of truth. A preliminary automated response is guidance only; an authorized person remains responsible for approvals, exceptions, and changes to official records.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to US-based employees eligible for Horizon Technologies benefit plans. Exact coverage is governed by official plan documents and applicable law.

When local law, an official benefit plan, a collective agreement, a customer obligation, or a written employment term provides a different requirement, the matter must be referred to Human Resources rather than resolved by assumption.

## 3. Definitions

### 3.1 Benefits-eligible employee

A regular employee scheduled for at least thirty hours per week unless a plan states otherwise.

### 3.2 Enrollment window

The period in which an eligible person may make benefit elections.

### 3.3 Qualifying life event

A recognized event that may permit a midyear election change.

### 3.4 Plan document

The official document controlling coverage, exclusions, and claims rights.

## 4. Policy Requirements

1. Benefits eligibility normally begins on the first day of the month following thirty days of eligible employment.
2. Newly eligible employees have thirty calendar days from the eligibility notice to complete elections.
3. Employees who do not enroll during the window normally wait until annual enrollment unless they experience a qualifying life event.
4. Marriage, divorce, birth, adoption, loss of other coverage, and certain employment changes may qualify, but documentation and deadlines apply.
5. A qualifying-life-event request should be submitted within thirty calendar days of the event unless the plan or law provides a different period.
6. The HR system's synthetic status is informational. Official carrier and plan records control when they differ.
7. The assistant must not diagnose medical needs, guarantee claims payment, or interpret ambiguous plan language as binding.

## 5. Request and Approval Process

1. The employee provides an employee ID and the benefit topic.
2. The system checks employment type, scheduled hours, eligibility date, and synthetic election status.
3. The agent retrieves policy evidence and identifies any missing event date or documentation.
4. Claims, coverage disputes, and plan-specific questions are escalated to Benefits or the plan administrator.

Requests should contain only information reasonably necessary for evaluation. The employee should receive a clear status: approved, denied with a policy-based reason, pending clarification, or escalated for specialist review.

## 6. Exceptions and Escalation

- Location-specific programs and collectively bargained benefits require specialist review.
- Conflicting status records must be escalated rather than resolved by guesswork.
- Sensitive medical information should not be requested unless necessary through an approved channel.

An exception is valid only when recorded by an authorized reviewer. Prior approval of a different request does not automatically create an exception for a new request.

## 7. Employee Responsibilities

- Review notices and deadlines.
- Provide accurate dependent and event information.
- Keep personal contact information current.
- Use secure channels for sensitive documents.

Employees are expected to provide accurate information and promptly correct material errors. Good-faith questions and requests for clarification are encouraged.

## 8. Manager and HR Responsibilities

- Direct employees to Benefits without requesting medical details.
- Allow reasonable time for required enrollment tasks.
- Avoid promising specific coverage or claim outcomes.

HR maintains policy interpretation, protects sensitive information, coordinates specialist review, and records approved exceptions. Managers must apply rules consistently and document operational decisions.

## 10. Examples

### 10.1 Standard example

A full-time employee reaches the eligibility date and has not elected coverage. The agent may explain the thirty-day enrollment window after checking the synthetic profile.

### 10.2 Clarification or escalation example

An employee asks whether a medical procedure will be paid. The agent should provide general navigation and escalate to the plan administrator rather than guarantee coverage.

Examples illustrate the policy but do not replace the requirements above. A real request must be evaluated using its own dates, employee data, location, evidence, and approval status.

## 11. Records, Privacy, and Automated Assistance

Records must be kept only in approved systems and limited to information needed for the business process. The HR assistant may retrieve evidence, summarize rules, check synthetic data, and create a mock action after confirmation. It may not make a legally binding decision, expose hidden reasoning, or claim that a mock action affected a real system.
