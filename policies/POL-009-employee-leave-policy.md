> This is a fictional policy created for an educational AI project. It does not describe a real company and does not provide legal, tax, immigration, medical, or financial advice.

## 1. Purpose

Explain how employees request and coordinate sick, medical, family, bereavement, civic, military, and other leaves without replacing applicable legal review.

This policy is intended to give employees, managers, and the HR support system a consistent source of truth. A preliminary automated response is guidance only; an authorized person remains responsible for approvals, exceptions, and changes to official records.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to Horizon Technologies employees in the United States. Eligibility and protections may vary by work location, tenure, hours, and reason for leave.

When local law, an official benefit plan, a collective agreement, a customer obligation, or a written employment term provides a different requirement, the matter must be referred to Human Resources rather than resolved by assumption.

## 3. Definitions

### 3.1 Leave

Approved time away that may be paid, unpaid, protected, or coordinated with another benefit.

### 3.2 Protected leave

Leave governed by an applicable law or approved accommodation process.

### 3.3 Continuous leave

Leave taken in one uninterrupted period.

### 3.4 Intermittent leave

Leave taken in separate blocks for an approved reason.

## 4. Policy Requirements

1. Employees should notify the company thirty calendar days before foreseeable leave or as soon as practicable when advance notice is not possible.
2. Employees may report a need for leave without giving detailed medical information to their manager. Required documentation goes through the approved HR channel.
3. PTO may coordinate with leave when allowed, but ordinary PTO approval does not determine legal leave eligibility.
4. Managers must refer potentially protected medical, family, pregnancy, disability, military, or civic-duty absences to HR.
5. Bereavement leave provides up to five paid workdays for an immediate family member and up to one paid workday for another significant relationship, with additional PTO or unpaid time subject to review.
6. Jury duty and qualifying military leave are handled according to applicable requirements and must be escalated for correct documentation and pay treatment.
7. The assistant may identify relevant policy sections but may not decide whether a condition is legally protected.

## 5. Request and Approval Process

1. The employee gives employee ID, general leave category, expected start date, and estimated duration.
2. HR determines applicable programs and sends secure documentation instructions.
3. The manager plans coverage using only the information needed for operations.
4. HR records approval, expected return, coordination with PTO or benefits, and any updates.

Requests should contain only information reasonably necessary for evaluation. The employee should receive a clear status: approved, denied with a policy-based reason, pending clarification, or escalated for specialist review.

## 6. Exceptions and Escalation

- Emergencies are reported as soon as practicable.
- Any indication of self-harm, immediate danger, violence, or urgent safety risk follows emergency and safety escalation rather than an ordinary leave workflow.
- Missing location, tenure, schedule, or reason category requires clarification or HR review.

An exception is valid only when recorded by an authorized reviewer. Prior approval of a different request does not automatically create an exception for a new request.

## 7. Employee Responsibilities

- Provide timely notice and required documentation through secure channels.
- Keep HR informed of material date changes.
- Follow return-to-work instructions where applicable.

Employees are expected to provide accurate information and promptly correct material errors. Good-faith questions and requests for clarification are encouraged.

## 8. Manager and HR Responsibilities

- Protect privacy.
- Refer potential protected leave promptly.
- Avoid interference or retaliation.
- Coordinate operational coverage without making legal determinations.

HR maintains policy interpretation, protects sensitive information, coordinates specialist review, and records approved exceptions. Managers must apply rules consistently and document operational decisions.

## 10. Examples

### 10.1 Standard example

An employee needs three days for vacation and has sufficient PTO. POL-001 controls the ordinary request.

### 10.2 Clarification or escalation example

An employee says they need recurring time away for treatment. The agent should avoid treating the request only as PTO and recommend confidential HR leave review.

Examples illustrate the policy but do not replace the requirements above. A real request must be evaluated using its own dates, employee data, location, evidence, and approval status.

Records must be kept only in approved systems and limited to information needed for the business process. It may not make a legally binding decision, expose hidden reasoning, or claim that a mock action affected a real system.
