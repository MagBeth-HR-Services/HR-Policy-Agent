> This is a fictional policy created for an educational AI project. It does not describe a real company and does not provide legal, tax, immigration, medical, or financial advice.

## 1. Purpose

Define the responsibilities and milestones required to prepare new employees for safe, productive, and compliant work.

This policy is intended to give employees, managers, and the HR support system a consistent source of truth. A preliminary automated response is guidance only; an authorized person remains responsible for approvals, exceptions, and changes to official records.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to newly hired employees, internal transfers requiring substantial onboarding, their managers, HR, IT, Security, and assigned onboarding partners.

When local law, an official benefit plan, a collective agreement, a customer obligation, or a written employment term provides a different requirement, the matter must be referred to Human Resources rather than resolved by assumption.

## 3. Definitions

### 3.1 Preboarding

Approved preparation before the first working day.

### 3.2 Onboarding plan

The role-specific schedule of access, training, meetings, and outcomes.

### 3.3 Required training

Training assigned because of law, policy, security, role, or customer obligation.

### 3.4 Access owner

The person authorized to approve access to a system or data set.

## 4. Policy Requirements

1. HR confirms identity, employment documents, work location, start date, manager, and employment type before access is provisioned.
2. IT issues the approved standard equipment and creates accounts based on an authorized request.
3. Managers provide a first-week schedule, role expectations, team introductions, and a thirty-, sixty-, and ninety-day plan.
4. New employees complete security, privacy, conduct, and role-required training by the assigned deadlines.
5. System access follows least privilege and may not be borrowed, shared, or approved by an unauthorized person.
6. Remote employees confirm equipment receipt, workspace readiness, connectivity, and approved work location.
7. Missing required documentation or failed security setup may delay access but must be handled consistently and without retaliation.

## 5. Request and Approval Process

1. HR opens the onboarding record after acceptance and required approvals.
2. The manager submits role and access needs.
3. IT and access owners provision only approved resources.
4. The employee completes acknowledgments and training.
5. The manager reviews progress at thirty, sixty, and ninety days and documents support needs.

Requests should contain only information reasonably necessary for evaluation. The employee should receive a clear status: approved, denied with a policy-based reason, pending clarification, or escalated for specialist review.

## 6. Exceptions and Escalation

- Urgent business need does not justify shared credentials or bypassed screening and security controls.
- Accessibility needs are coordinated confidentially with HR.
- International location or employment questions require specialist review before a start date is confirmed.

An exception is valid only when recorded by an authorized reviewer. Prior approval of a different request does not automatically create an exception for a new request.

## 7. Employee Responsibilities

- Complete accurate forms and training.
- Protect issued credentials and equipment.
- Ask questions when instructions or access are unclear.
- Report location or personal-data corrections promptly.

Employees are expected to provide accurate information and promptly correct material errors. Good-faith questions and requests for clarification are encouraged.

## 8. Manager and HR Responsibilities

- Plan meaningful work and support.
- Request only necessary access.
- Give timely feedback.
- Escalate missing resources or accommodation needs appropriately.

HR maintains policy interpretation, protects sensitive information, coordinates specialist review, and records approved exceptions. Managers must apply rules consistently and document operational decisions.

## 10. Examples

### 10.1 Standard example

A remote new hire receives equipment before the start date, completes MFA setup, and follows the scheduled orientation plan.

### 10.2 Clarification or escalation example

A manager asks a new employee to use a colleague's credentials while access is pending. The request violates security and onboarding rules and must be refused and escalated.

Examples illustrate the policy but do not replace the requirements above. A real request must be evaluated using its own dates, employee data, location, evidence, and approval status.

## 11. Records, Privacy, and Automated Assistance

Records must be kept only in approved systems and limited to information needed for the business process. The HR assistant may retrieve evidence, summarize rules, check synthetic data, and create a mock action after confirmation. It may not make a legally binding decision, expose hidden reasoning, or claim that a mock action affected a real system.
