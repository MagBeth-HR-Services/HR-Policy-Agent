> This is a fictional policy created for an educational AI project. It does not describe a real company and does not provide legal, tax, immigration, medical, or financial advice.

## 1. Purpose

Establish consistent rules for earning, requesting, approving, using, and recording paid time off while maintaining reliable team coverage.

This policy is intended to give employees, managers, and the HR support system a consistent source of truth. A preliminary automated response is guidance only; an authorized person remains responsible for approvals, exceptions, and changes to official records.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to US-based regular full-time and eligible part-time employees. Contractors, agency workers, temporary workers, and interns do not earn PTO unless a written program states otherwise. Employees covered by a location-specific rule receive whichever protection is greater.

When local law, an official benefit plan, a collective agreement, a customer obligation, or a written employment term provides a different requirement, the matter must be referred to Human Resources rather than resolved by assumption.

## 3. Definitions

### 3.1 PTO

Paid time that an eligible employee may use for vacation, personal matters, or short-term illness.

### 3.2 Available balance

Accrued PTO shown in the synthetic HR record, less approved future requests.

### 3.3 Business day

A scheduled workday that is not a Horizon Technologies company holiday.

### 3.4 PTO request

A request submitted with employee ID, dates, hours, and any coverage information required by the manager.

## 4. Policy Requirements

1. Regular full-time employees accrue 160 PTO hours per calendar year in equal pay-period increments. Eligible part-time employees accrue a prorated amount based on their standard weekly schedule.
2. PTO may be used only after it appears in the employee's available balance. Negative balances and borrowing from future accruals are not permitted without a documented HR exception.
3. Requests for one or two consecutive business days should be submitted at least five calendar days in advance. Requests for three to five consecutive business days should be submitted at least fourteen calendar days in advance. Requests longer than five consecutive business days should be submitted at least thirty calendar days in advance.
4. Unplanned illness or emergency absence may be reported as soon as practicable and does not require advance notice, but the employee must follow the team's call-out process.
5. A company holiday occurring inside an approved PTO period is recorded as holiday time, not PTO, when the employee is eligible for that holiday.
6. PTO requests require manager approval. Approval depends on available balance, required notice, operational coverage, overlapping approved absences, and any documented business-critical period.
7. Managers must apply coverage rules consistently and may not deny a request for a discriminatory or retaliatory reason. When multiple requests conflict, managers consider submission time, critical skills, prior accommodations, and reasonable alternatives.
8. Unused PTO of up to 40 hours carries into the next calendar year. The maximum bank is 200 hours; accrual pauses while the balance is at the maximum.

## 5. Request and Approval Process

1. The employee submits their employee ID, requested dates, total hours, and known coverage concerns through the mock HR workflow.
2. The system checks the employee profile, employment type, available balance, company holidays, and the applicable notice period.
3. The manager reviews coverage and records approval or denial. A denial must include a policy-based reason and, when feasible, an alternative date.
4. The request is not final until approval is recorded. The employee should not make non-refundable plans based only on a preliminary policy check.

Requests should contain only information reasonably necessary for evaluation. The employee should receive a clear status: approved, denied with a policy-based reason, pending clarification, or escalated for specialist review.

## 6. Exceptions and Escalation

- Escalate to HR when an employee ID is missing, the employment type is unclear, records conflict, a negative balance is requested, or a location-specific leave law may apply.
- Medical, parental, military, disability-related, and other protected leave questions must be evaluated under POL-009 and escalated when appropriate rather than treated only as ordinary PTO.
- The agent may provide preliminary guidance but may not approve or deny PTO, change a balance, or create a record without explicit confirmation.

An exception is valid only when recorded by an authorized reviewer. Prior approval of a different request does not automatically create an exception for a new request.

## 7. Employee Responsibilities

- Maintain an accurate employee ID and request record.
- Provide the required notice whenever practicable.
- Coordinate reasonable handoffs without disclosing private medical details.
- Verify approval before beginning planned leave.

Employees are expected to provide accurate information and promptly correct material errors. Good-faith questions and requests for clarification are encouraged.

## 8. Manager and HR Responsibilities

- Review requests promptly and consistently.
- Validate coverage rather than asking for unnecessary personal details.
- Document policy-based reasons for denial.
- Escalate uncertain or protected-leave situations to HR.

HR maintains policy interpretation, protects sensitive information, coordinates specialist review, and records approved exceptions. Managers must apply rules consistently and document operational decisions.

Related policies should be retrieved together when a request spans more than one subject. If evidence conflicts, the agent must identify the conflict and escalate rather than select the more convenient rule.

## 10. Examples

### 10.1 Standard example

Employee E1001 has 56 available PTO hours and requests 24 hours across three business days beginning sixteen calendar days from today. No holiday overlaps. The balance and notice rules are satisfied, so the agent may report that the request appears eligible but still requires manager approval.

### 10.2 Clarification or escalation example

An employee requests five days beginning tomorrow and has only sixteen available hours. The agent must not claim approval. It should explain the balance and notice conflicts and recommend clarification or HR review if an emergency or protected leave may apply.

Examples illustrate the policy but do not replace the requirements above. A real request must be evaluated using its own dates, employee data, location, evidence, and approval status.

## 11. Records, Privacy, and Automated Assistance

Records must be kept only in approved systems and limited to information needed for the business process. The HR assistant may retrieve evidence, summarize rules, check synthetic data, and create a mock action after confirmation. It may not make a legally binding decision, expose hidden reasoning, or claim that a mock action affected a real system.
