> This is a fictional policy created for an educational AI project. It does not describe a real company and does not provide legal, tax, immigration, medical, or financial advice.

## 1. Purpose

Establish review requirements for employees who want to work temporarily from a state or country other than their approved primary work location.

This policy is intended to give employees, managers, and the HR support system a consistent source of truth. A preliminary automated response is guidance only; an authorized person remains responsible for approvals, exceptions, and changes to official records.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies whenever an employee will perform work from a temporary domestic or international location. Personal travel with no work performed is outside scope.

When local law, an official benefit plan, a collective agreement, a customer obligation, or a written employment term provides a different requirement, the matter must be referred to Human Resources rather than resolved by assumption.

## 3. Definitions

### 3.1 Domestic temporary work

Work from a US state or territory different from the recorded primary work location.

### 3.2 International temporary work

Work performed outside the employee's country of employment.

### 3.3 Restricted location

A location prohibited or limited because of sanctions, security, customer contracts, insurance, tax, immigration, or employment rules.

### 3.4 Approval window

The exact dates and location authorized for temporary work.

## 4. Policy Requirements

1. Employees must receive written approval before working from a different country and before domestic temporary work exceeding five business days.
2. International requests should be submitted at least forty-five calendar days before departure. Domestic requests longer than five business days should be submitted at least twenty-one calendar days in advance.
3. International work is normally limited to twenty business days in a rolling twelve-month period. A longer request, including six weeks, requires HR, Legal, Tax, Security, and manager review and may be denied.
4. Approval is location-specific and date-specific. Approval for one trip does not create standing permission for future trips or nearby countries.
5. Employees may not work from restricted locations or use temporary work to change their employment residence, payroll location, or immigration status.
6. The employee must remain authorized to enter and stay in the destination. Horizon Technologies does not provide personal immigration or tax advice through the HR assistant.
7. Roles handling export-controlled data, regulated personal data, production credentials, government information, or customer-restricted data require enhanced Security and Legal review.
8. Working hours must maintain essential team and customer coverage. Material time-zone differences require a documented schedule.
9. The company may revoke approval when laws, security conditions, customer obligations, business needs, or employee circumstances change.

## 5. Request and Approval Process

1. The employee supplies employee ID, destination city and country or state, exact dates, reason, expected working hours, data-access needs, and equipment plan.
2. The manager reviews business coverage and performance suitability.
3. HR confirms employment type and primary location; Security assesses systems and destination risk.
4. International or elevated-risk requests are routed to Legal and Tax reviewers.
5. Approval is recorded only after every required reviewer responds.

Requests should contain only information reasonably necessary for evaluation. The employee should receive a clear status: approved, denied with a policy-based reason, pending clarification, or escalated for specialist review.

## 6. Exceptions and Escalation

- Emergency displacement may receive expedited review but is not automatically approved.
- A request longer than twenty international business days always requires escalation and must not be represented as compliant by the agent.
- Missing destination, dates, employee ID, or primary location requires clarification before assessment.

An exception is valid only when recorded by an authorized reviewer. Prior approval of a different request does not automatically create an exception for a new request.

## 7. Employee Responsibilities

- Request review before booking plans that depend on working approval.
- Provide exact and truthful location information.
- Follow destination-specific security instructions.
- Stop work and contact the company if travel or legal status changes.

Employees are expected to provide accurate information and promptly correct material errors. Good-faith questions and requests for clarification are encouraged.

## 8. Manager and HR Responsibilities

- Evaluate coverage and job suitability.
- Avoid promising approval before specialist review.
- Notify HR of any material change.
- Provide an operational recommendation without giving legal or tax advice.

HR maintains policy interpretation, protects sensitive information, coordinates specialist review, and records approved exceptions. Managers must apply rules consistently and document operational decisions.

## 10. Examples

### 10.1 Standard example

An employee asks to work from another US state for three business days while visiting family. If the location is not restricted and the manager approves coverage, the routine short domestic rule may apply.

### 10.2 Clarification or escalation example

Employee E1002 asks to work from another country for six weeks. Because the duration exceeds twenty business days, the agent must cite this policy, retrieve remote-work and security evidence, and recommend formal HR review rather than approval.

Examples illustrate the policy but do not replace the requirements above. A real request must be evaluated using its own dates, employee data, location, evidence, and approval status.

It may not make a legally binding decision, expose hidden reasoning, or claim that a mock action affected a real system.
