> This is a fictional policy created for an educational AI project. It does not describe a real company and does not provide legal, tax, immigration, medical, or financial advice.

## 1. Purpose

Define which reasonable and necessary business costs may be reimbursed and the evidence and approvals required.

This policy is intended to give employees, managers, and the HR support system a consistent source of truth. A preliminary automated response is guidance only; an authorized person remains responsible for approvals, exceptions, and changes to official records.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to employees incurring authorized expenses for Horizon Technologies business. It does not cover payroll, personal purchases, or contractor invoices.

When local law, an official benefit plan, a collective agreement, a customer obligation, or a written employment term provides a different requirement, the matter must be referred to Human Resources rather than resolved by assumption.

## 3. Definitions

### 3.1 Business purpose

A specific explanation connecting an expense to company work.

### 3.2 Itemized receipt

A receipt showing vendor, date, items, taxes, and amount.

### 3.3 Preapproval

Documented approval obtained before committing company funds.

### 3.4 Reimbursement request

A submission containing expense details, evidence, business purpose, and required approval.

## 4. Policy Requirements

1. Expenses must be reasonable, necessary, lawful, and connected to an approved business purpose.
2. Itemized receipts are required for expenses of 25 dollars or more and whenever requested by Finance.
3. Requests should be submitted within thirty calendar days and no later than sixty calendar days after the expense.
4. Airfare, lodging, conferences, and single purchases of 500 dollars or more require preapproval.
5. Home-office furniture requires an approved remote-work arrangement and manager preapproval; the standard reimbursement limit is 400 dollars once every three years.
6. Laptops and core computing equipment must normally be ordered through IT under POL-007 rather than purchased personally.
7. Alcohol, personal entertainment, fines, upgrades for personal preference, companion travel, and commuting to the regular workplace are not reimbursable unless an explicit exception applies.
8. The employee may not divide a purchase to avoid an approval threshold.

## 5. Request and Approval Process

1. Submit date, vendor, amount, currency, category, business purpose, receipt, and approval evidence.
2. The manager confirms necessity and budget; Finance checks evidence and policy limits.
3. Returned requests identify missing information and may be resubmitted.
4. Approved reimbursements follow the normal payment cycle.

Requests should contain only information reasonably necessary for evaluation. The employee should receive a clear status: approved, denied with a policy-based reason, pending clarification, or escalated for specialist review.

## 6. Exceptions and Escalation

- Lost receipts require a signed explanation and Finance review.
- International expenses require the original currency and payment evidence.
- The agent may provide a preliminary eligibility result but cannot promise reimbursement or alter financial records.

An exception is valid only when recorded by an authorized reviewer. Prior approval of a different request does not automatically create an exception for a new request.

## 7. Employee Responsibilities

- Seek preapproval when required.
- Choose reasonable options.
- Retain accurate records.
- Repay duplicate or erroneous reimbursements.

Employees are expected to provide accurate information and promptly correct material errors. Good-faith questions and requests for clarification are encouraged.

## 8. Manager and HR Responsibilities

- Review business purpose and budget consistently.
- Avoid approving personal or inadequately documented costs.
- Escalate unusual, executive, or conflict-of-interest expenses.

HR maintains policy interpretation, protects sensitive information, coordinates specialist review, and records approved exceptions. Managers must apply rules consistently and document operational decisions.

Related policies should be retrieved together when a request spans more than one subject. If evidence conflicts, the agent must identify the conflict and escalate rather than select the more convenient rule.

## 10. Examples

### 10.1 Standard example

An employee with an approved remote arrangement buys a 325-dollar ergonomic chair after written manager preapproval and submits an itemized receipt within thirty days. The request appears within the standard limit.

### 10.2 Clarification or escalation example

An employee personally buys a laptop without IT approval. The agent should retrieve both expense and equipment policies and explain that reimbursement is not automatically allowed.

Examples illustrate the policy but do not replace the requirements above. A real request must be evaluated using its own dates, employee data, location, evidence, and approval status.

## 11. Records, Privacy, and Automated Assistance

Records must be kept only in approved systems and limited to information needed for the business process. The HR assistant may retrieve evidence, summarize rules, check synthetic data, and create a mock action after confirmation. It may not make a legally binding decision, expose hidden reasoning, or claim that a mock action affected a real system.
