> This is a fictional policy created for an educational AI project. It does not describe a real company and does not provide legal, tax, immigration, medical, or financial advice.

## 1. Purpose

Set expectations for respectful, ethical, inclusive, and safe conduct and provide reporting and non-retaliation principles.

This policy is intended to give employees, managers, and the HR support system a consistent source of truth. A preliminary automated response is guidance only; an authorized person remains responsible for approvals, exceptions, and changes to official records.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to employees and contingent workers in offices, remote settings, work travel, online communications, company events, and interactions connected to Horizon Technologies.

When local law, an official benefit plan, a collective agreement, a customer obligation, or a written employment term provides a different requirement, the matter must be referred to Human Resources rather than resolved by assumption.

## 3. Definitions

### 3.1 Harassment

Unwelcome conduct that violates policy or applicable standards because of its nature, severity, persistence, or protected basis.

### 3.2 Retaliation

Adverse treatment because a person raised a concern, requested help, participated in a review, or exercised a protected right.

### 3.3 Conflict of interest

A personal interest that may impair or appear to impair objective business judgment.

### 3.4 Good-faith report

A sincere report made without knowingly providing false information.

## 4. Policy Requirements

1. Everyone must communicate professionally and treat others with dignity across in-person and digital channels.
2. Discrimination, harassment, threats, bullying, retaliation, and deliberate humiliation are prohibited.
3. Employees must disclose actual or potential conflicts of interest and must not use company information or authority for improper personal benefit.
4. Confidential concerns should be shared only with people who need the information for response or support.
5. Managers who receive a possible harassment, discrimination, retaliation, safety, or ethics concern must promptly contact HR even when the employee requests no action.
6. Good-faith reporting is protected. Knowingly false statements may be addressed, but an unsubstantiated concern is not automatically a bad-faith report.
7. The HR assistant must not investigate, determine credibility, promise confidentiality, or decide discipline.

## 5. Request and Approval Process

1. Employees may report concerns to a manager, HR, the designated ethics channel, or Security when appropriate.
2. Reports should identify the general concern, relevant dates, and immediate safety needs without requiring the employee to confront another person.
3. Qualified reviewers assess scope, interim protections, privacy, and next steps.
4. Outcomes are shared only to the extent appropriate and permitted.

Requests should contain only information reasonably necessary for evaluation. The employee should receive a clear status: approved, denied with a policy-based reason, pending clarification, or escalated for specialist review.

## 6. Exceptions and Escalation

- Immediate danger, violence, or medical emergency requires local emergency services and company safety escalation.
- The agent should create only a mock summary after explicit confirmation and must avoid collecting unnecessary sensitive details.
- A request for legal conclusions or guaranteed outcomes must be redirected to qualified reviewers.

An exception is valid only when recorded by an authorized reviewer. Prior approval of a different request does not automatically create an exception for a new request.

## 7. Employee Responsibilities

- Follow conduct standards.
- Raise concerns in good faith.
- Preserve relevant information.
- Respect privacy and avoid retaliation.

Employees are expected to provide accurate information and promptly correct material errors. Good-faith questions and requests for clarification are encouraged.

## 8. Manager and HR Responsibilities

- Listen without promising a particular outcome.
- Address immediate safety.
- Escalate promptly.
- Protect against retaliation and unnecessary disclosure.

HR maintains policy interpretation, protects sensitive information, coordinates specialist review, and records approved exceptions. Managers must apply rules consistently and document operational decisions.

## 9. Related Policies

Related policies should be retrieved together when a request spans more than one subject. If evidence conflicts, the agent must identify the conflict and escalate rather than select the more convenient rule.

## 10. Examples

### 10.1 Standard example

An employee reports repeated demeaning messages. The agent should provide reporting options and offer a mock case summary after confirmation, not decide whether harassment occurred.

### 10.2 Clarification or escalation example

A user describes an immediate threat. The agent should prioritize emergency and safety guidance rather than continue a routine policy workflow.

Examples illustrate the policy but do not replace the requirements above. A real request must be evaluated using its own dates, employee data, location, evidence, and approval status.

## 11. Records, Privacy, and Automated Assistance

Records must be kept only in approved systems and limited to information needed for the business process. The HR assistant may retrieve evidence, summarize rules, check synthetic data, and create a mock action after confirmation. It may not make a legally binding decision, expose hidden reasoning, or claim that a mock action affected a real system.
