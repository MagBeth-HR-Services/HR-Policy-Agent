> This is a fictional policy created for an educational AI project. It does not describe a real company and does not provide legal, tax, immigration, medical, or financial advice.

## 1. Purpose

Define company holidays, eligibility, scheduling expectations, and the interaction between holiday pay and other time-off categories.

This policy is intended to give employees, managers, and the HR support system a consistent source of truth. A preliminary automated response is guidance only; an authorized person remains responsible for approvals, exceptions, and changes to official records.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to US-based regular employees. Holiday schedules for employees assigned outside the United States are established by their employing entity and require HR confirmation.

When local law, an official benefit plan, a collective agreement, a customer obligation, or a written employment term provides a different requirement, the matter must be referred to Human Resources rather than resolved by assumption.

## 3. Definitions

### 3.1 Company holiday

A date designated by Horizon Technologies as a paid closure or reduced-operations day.

### 3.2 Essential coverage

Work that must continue to protect customers, systems, safety, or legal obligations.

### 3.3 Floating holiday

One paid day selected by an eligible employee with manager approval.

## 4. Policy Requirements

1. Horizon Technologies observes New Year's Day, Martin Luther King Jr. Day, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving Day, the Friday after Thanksgiving, and Christmas Day for eligible US employees.
2. When a fixed-date holiday falls on Saturday, it is normally observed Friday; when it falls on Sunday, it is normally observed Monday. HR publishes the authoritative annual calendar.
3. Regular full-time employees receive eight hours of holiday pay. Eligible part-time employees receive holiday hours proportional to their regular schedule for that weekday.
4. A company holiday inside approved PTO is not deducted from the employee's PTO balance. Employees must not submit duplicate PTO hours for the same scheduled hours.
5. Teams providing essential coverage may use rotating schedules. Employees required to work receive an alternate paid day within sixty days, subject to manager approval and applicable law.
6. Each regular employee receives one floating holiday per calendar year after thirty days of employment. It does not carry over and has no cash value except where required by law.

## 5. Request and Approval Process

1. HR publishes the annual calendar before December 1 of the prior year.
2. Employees request floating holidays using the normal time-off process at least five calendar days in advance.
3. Managers document essential-coverage assignments and alternate-day arrangements.

Requests should contain only information reasonably necessary for evaluation. The employee should receive a clear status: approved, denied with a policy-based reason, pending clarification, or escalated for specialist review.

## 6. Exceptions and Escalation

- Escalate discrepancies between a work location and the published calendar to HR.
- Religious or disability-related accommodation requests follow the accommodation process and must not be dismissed as ordinary scheduling preferences.
- The agent may explain calendar rules but cannot change pay or attendance records.

An exception is valid only when recorded by an authorized reviewer. Prior approval of a different request does not automatically create an exception for a new request.

## 7. Employee Responsibilities

- Review the calendar for the assigned work location.
- Avoid submitting PTO for an eligible holiday.
- Confirm alternate-day arrangements in writing when assigned essential coverage.

Employees are expected to provide accurate information and promptly correct material errors. Good-faith questions and requests for clarification are encouraged.

## 8. Manager and HR Responsibilities

- Plan essential coverage fairly.
- Record alternate paid days.
- Refer accommodation requests to HR without requesting unnecessary sensitive details.

HR maintains policy interpretation, protects sensitive information, coordinates specialist review, and records approved exceptions. Managers must apply rules consistently and document operational decisions.

## 10. Examples

### 10.1 Standard example

An approved three-day PTO period includes Thanksgiving. Only the two non-holiday business days are deducted from PTO.

### 10.2 Clarification or escalation example

A remote employee follows the calendar associated with the employing work location in the HR profile, not the holiday calendar of a temporary travel destination.

Examples illustrate the policy but do not replace the requirements above. A real request must be evaluated using its own dates, employee data, location, evidence, and approval status.

## 11. Records, Privacy, and Automated Assistance

Records must be kept only in approved systems and limited to information needed for the business process. The HR assistant may retrieve evidence, summarize rules, check synthetic data, and create a mock action after confirmation. It may not make a legally binding decision, expose hidden reasoning, or claim that a mock action affected a real system.
