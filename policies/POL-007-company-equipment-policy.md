> This is a fictional policy created for an educational AI project. It does not describe a real company and does not provide legal, tax, immigration, medical, or financial advice.

## 1. Purpose

Define eligibility, ordering, use, care, support, replacement, security, and return requirements for company equipment.

This policy is intended to give employees, managers, and the HR support system a consistent source of truth. A preliminary automated response is guidance only; an authorized person remains responsible for approvals, exceptions, and changes to official records.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to employees and approved contingent workers issued equipment or authorized to purchase accessories for Horizon Technologies work.

When local law, an official benefit plan, a collective agreement, a customer obligation, or a written employment term provides a different requirement, the matter must be referred to Human Resources rather than resolved by assumption.

## 3. Definitions

### 3.1 Company equipment

Hardware, accessories, devices, or other assets purchased or owned by Horizon Technologies.

### 3.2 Standard kit

Role-based equipment approved by IT and Procurement.

### 3.3 Asset record

The inventory record connecting equipment to a person and location.

### 3.4 Accessory allowance

A limited approved amount for eligible peripherals that are not centrally issued.

## 4. Policy Requirements

1. IT assigns a standard kit based on role, accessibility needs, security profile, and work arrangement.
2. Employees must not purchase laptops, phones, storage devices, or networking equipment for reimbursement without written IT and manager preapproval.
3. Company equipment is primarily for authorized business use and must not be shared with unauthorized people.
4. Employees must not remove management controls, install prohibited software, or make unapproved hardware modifications.
5. Loss, theft, damage, or suspected compromise must be reported immediately to IT and Security.
6. Normal wear is repaired or replaced by IT. Damage caused by reckless or intentional misuse may be reviewed under applicable rules and law.
7. Equipment may not be taken internationally until the temporary-location and security reviews are complete.
8. All equipment must be returned by the stated deadline when employment ends, a role changes, or IT requests replacement.

## 5. Request and Approval Process

1. The employee or manager submits the role, equipment need, work location, and business justification.
2. IT selects approved equipment and records the asset assignment.
3. Support requests use the designated service channel.
4. Returned devices are inventoried, securely processed, and either reassigned or disposed of.

Requests should contain only information reasonably necessary for evaluation. The employee should receive a clear status: approved, denied with a policy-based reason, pending clarification, or escalated for specialist review.

## 6. Exceptions and Escalation

- Accessibility-related equipment requests are coordinated with HR and handled confidentially.
- Emergency local purchases require documented IT authorization.
- The agent may explain eligibility but cannot authorize a purchase or mark equipment returned.

An exception is valid only when recorded by an authorized reviewer. Prior approval of a different request does not automatically create an exception for a new request.

## 7. Employee Responsibilities

- Protect and maintain assigned assets.
- Keep location and contact information current.
- Use approved support channels.
- Return equipment promptly and completely.

Employees are expected to provide accurate information and promptly correct material errors. Good-faith questions and requests for clarification are encouraged.

## 8. Manager and HR Responsibilities

- Confirm legitimate role needs.
- Avoid promising nonstandard equipment before IT review.
- Coordinate equipment recovery during transitions.

HR maintains policy interpretation, protects sensitive information, coordinates specialist review, and records approved exceptions. Managers must apply rules consistently and document operational decisions.

## 10. Examples

A new remote employee receives a standard laptop, monitor, keyboard, and headset through IT as part of onboarding.

### 10.2 Clarification or escalation example

An employee wants to buy a premium laptop personally. The agent should explain that manager approval alone is insufficient because IT preapproval and asset controls are required.

Examples illustrate the policy but do not replace the requirements above. A real request must be evaluated using its own dates, employee data, location, evidence, and approval status.
